# Teaching Notes: Diffraction of Waves

## Core Concepts
- **Diffraction:** The spreading of waves when they pass through a gap or around an obstacle.
- **Constant Wavelength:** Diffraction does NOT change the speed, frequency, or wavelength of the wave (assuming the medium remains the same). Spacing between crests must remain constant (Passages D01, D03, D05).
- **Effect of Gap Size:** 
  - Narrower gap = more spreading/diffraction (curved wavefronts).
  - Wider gap = less spreading/diffraction (wavefronts are flatter, but NOT perfectly flat) (Passages D02, D06).

## Drawing Guidelines for Exams
- Draw curved, approximately circular wave crests after the gap.
- Draw at least three crests.
- Always use a ruler to ensure the spacing (wavelength) between crests is exactly the same before and after the gap.
- Wave crests bend into the shadow region (Passages D01, D04, D05).

## Common Misconceptions to Correct
- *Misconception:* Wavelength increases/decreases after the gap. *Correction:* No, wavelength remains identical.
- *Misconception:* A wider gap causes more diffraction. *Correction:* No, a wider gap causes LESS diffraction.
- *Misconception:* Wavefronts become perfectly straight/flat after a wide gap. *Correction:* They become *flatter*, but retain some curvature. They are never completely flat.

## Exam Strategy
- When an examiner asks for one mistake in a diagram (for 1 mark), provide exactly one clear, valid criticism (e.g., "wavelength is too large" or "crests are too straight"). Do not provide extra, conflicting information (Passage D04).
